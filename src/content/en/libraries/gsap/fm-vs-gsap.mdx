## In one line {#tldr}

- **Framer Motion** … animation that **stays close to React state**. Mount, hover, tap, and enter/exit (`AnimatePresence`), written declaratively on the component.
- **GSAP** … animation that **designs the motion itself**. Involved timelines, scroll-linked motion (ScrollTrigger's scrub / pin), fine-grained control.

Both are excellent, and the trick is **not trying to do everything with one of them**. Pick the one closer to what you want to build.

## Quick reference {#table}

| What you want to build | The one suited for it |
| --- | --- |
| A list fades in / lifts on hover | Framer Motion |
| Smooth enter/exit on add and remove | Framer Motion (`AnimatePresence`) |
| Interaction feedback on buttons and cards | Framer Motion (`whileHover` / `whileTap`) |
| Flow several motions in order and with overlap | GSAP (`timeline`) |
| Move / pin things to the scroll amount | GSAP (`ScrollTrigger`'s scrub / pin) |
| An elaborate key-visual effect for an LP | GSAP |

## Tips for choosing {#how-to-choose}

1. **First, "does it really need to move?"** … Simple color or size changes are often enough with a CSS `transition` ([the "motion needs a reason" point we touched on in the Framer Motion goal](/libraries/framer-motion/goal)).
2. **If it's tied to React state, Framer Motion** … Motion that follows a `state` change — "open/closed," "favorited" — is most natural with Framer Motion.
3. **If it's scroll or an involved order, GSAP** … If you need scroll-linked motion, timelines, or fine playback control, reach for GSAP.

<Callout type="note" title="Mixing is fine">

You're free to use both in one app. It's natural to split them by role: **a component's visual motion with Framer Motion, the whole page's scroll effects with GSAP**. Just avoid driving the same single element with both at once, since they'll fight over it.

</Callout>

## Our standard {#standard}

<Std
  decision="Component-level motion (mount, hover, enter/exit) goes to Framer Motion. Scroll-linked motion and involved timelines go to GSAP"
  alt="For a small project with little motion, Framer Motion alone gets the job done. Conversely, for a flashy scroll-driven LP, GSAP takes center stage. Lean toward whichever fits the project's character."
>

Keep both installed and pick **whichever is closer to the motion you want to build**. When in doubt, think "UI motion tied to state → Framer Motion, scroll or effects → GSAP," and it's easy to decide.

</Std>
