<PartIntro>

**GSAP** (GreenSock Animation Platform) is the go-to library for web animation. The **polished effects** you often see on LPs — "elements move as you scroll," "several movements play one after another" — can be built with fine-grained control.

The good news: as of **2025, every GSAP feature is free** (including plugins like ScrollTrigger that used to be paid). From React, you can use it safely through the official **`@gsap/react`** (the `useGSAP` hook).

</PartIntro>

## What GSAP is good at {#why}

[Framer Motion](/libraries/framer-motion/goal) was good at "animating in sync with React state" (mount, hover, enter/exit). GSAP goes one step further: it's good at **designing the movement itself in detail**.

- **Timeline** … lay out **order and overlap** — "B after A, C slightly overlapping" — along a time axis.
- **ScrollTrigger** … **tie** movement to scroll position (reveal when it comes into view, move in step with scroll amount = scrub, hold it in place = pin). This is the star of LPs.
- **Fine control** … easing (the pace of movement), repeats, reverse, starting partway through, and more — the timeline is yours to command.

For something like "a list fading in gently," Framer Motion is plenty. Reach for GSAP when you need **scroll-linked motion or intricate, ordered effects** — that's the division of labor (more on the [when-to-use-which page](/libraries/gsap/fm-vs-gsap)).

## Give motion a "reason," and be considerate {#restraint}

GSAP is powerful, so it's easy to overdo it. Following the same principle as the [Framer Motion part](/libraries/framer-motion/goal), **give motion a reason** (drawing attention, signaling a state change, responding to an action). And GSAP handles **consideration for `prefers-reduced-motion` (the reduce-motion setting)** from the start via `gsap.matchMedia()`. Every demo in this part builds in that consideration.
