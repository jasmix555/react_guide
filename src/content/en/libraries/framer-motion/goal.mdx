## Give every movement a "reason" {#why}

An important premise: **more animation is not better**. When things are constantly moving all over the place, it's noisy, hard to read, and can even make some people queasy. Whenever you add movement, always give it a **reason**.

- **Draw attention** … a newly appearing element fades in to catch the eye.
- **Communicate a change of state** … show additions, removals, and switches smoothly instead of abruptly.
- **Respond to interaction** … return a bit of movement when something is pressed or hovered.

Don't animate just "because it looks kind of cool" — that's the trick to not being noisy. And we'll handle **care for people who are sensitive to motion** (`prefers-reduced-motion`) from the very start of this part.

## How this part is structured {#roadmap}

1. **motion basics** — `motion.div` and `initial` / `animate` / `transition`.
2. **Hover and tap** — respond to interaction with `whileHover` / `whileTap`.
3. **Reduced-motion setting** — use `useReducedMotion` to care for people who are sensitive to motion.
4. **Scroll and stagger** — reveal when visible, and reveal a little at a time.
5. **Enter/exit animation** — with `AnimatePresence`, make things smooth when they disappear too.
6. **Capstone** — add movement to the list of `ProductCard`s.

Each page comes with the **full code** and a working **demo**. Try them out and see for yourself where "feels good" ends and "too much" begins.
