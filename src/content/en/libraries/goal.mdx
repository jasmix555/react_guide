## Libraries are "ready-made parts" {#what}

A library is a **ready-made part that someone else built and published**. Formatting dates nicely, adding animation, assembling class names — for these kinds of "common tasks," you can borrow someone's work instead of writing it from scratch.

In LP work, you loaded and used jQuery plugins and sliders too. It's the same idea. The difference is that in React, instead of loading them with a `<script>` tag, you **bring them into your own project with `npm install`**.

<Callout type="analogy">

In cooking terms, it's the choice between making stock from scratch or using a store-bought stock pack. Store-bought items (libraries) are fast and reliable, but if you pile too many onto the shelf, managing them gets hard. The trick is to use only as many as you can keep track of — knowing "what's in there."

</Callout>

## Be able to say "why" before you add it {#why}

The most important thing in this chapter isn't a technique — it's an **attitude**. **Before adding a part, stop once and ask "do I really need this?"** — just that alone dramatically reduces trouble down the line.

There are two reasons. One is that once you add a part, you're **committed to maintaining it forever** (updates, bugs, compatibility with other parts). The other is that **the standard features, or parts you already have, are often enough**. "Turns out I didn't need that library" happens all the time.

That's why, before covering individual libraries, this chapter starts with **how to choose them, and what changes when you add one**.
