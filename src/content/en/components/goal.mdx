## React "builds screens by combining parts" {#idea}

React's core idea is to **split a screen into small parts and combine them**. Buttons, product cards, search boxes... you build each one as an independent part (a **component**) and assemble them like Lego into a single screen.

<Callout type="analogy">

Think back to PHP's `include` that you used for LP work (putting the header and footer in separate files and pulling them in). React takes that further and **turns even a single button or a single card into a part**. On top of that, you can pass values to a part (the "arguments" of `include`) to change what's inside.

</Callout>

Once you've built a part, **you only fix it in one place**. Want to move the price on every card? Just fix the card part once. Even with 100 cards, the change applies to all of them. That's the decisive difference from hand-copying the same look over and over.

## How this part is structured {#roadmap}

Working top to bottom, we'll assemble the parts little by little.

1. **JSX** -- the mechanism for writing HTML-like markup inside React.
2. **Your first component** -- build one part and put it on screen.
3. **props** -- pass values to a part to change what's inside.
4. **Rendering a list (map)** -- lay out a part once per piece of data. The `key` story.
5. **Conditional rendering** -- change what shows based on things like stock.
6. **Events** -- respond to clicks.
7. **Reuse (variants)** -- gather look-and-feel variations into one part.

By the end, you'll be able to assemble a list like the demo at the top with your own hands.
